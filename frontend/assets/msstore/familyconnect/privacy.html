Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 September 2026 · Applies to: Family Connect for Windows — the family messenger published by nettrash on the Microsoft Store, for Windows 11 (x64 and ARM64). The same service is described for the iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android apps on their own page; what happens on the server is identical, and this page adds what is particular to Windows.

This policy is versioned alongside the app's source code; the most recent commit on master is authoritative.

Read this part first: there are two different situations

Family Connect is self-hosted software. Your family's messages live on one server, and who runs that server decides who can read them. There are two cases, and they are genuinely different:

If you… Then…
installed from the Microsoft Store and did not change the server address Your app is connected to https://fc.nettrash.me, which I operate. I hold your messages, photos, videos, voice notes, files, shared locations, profile picture and account details, and I am technically able to read all of them. For you, I am the data controller.
run your own server (“Change server” on the sign-in screen, before you sign in) I receive nothing at all. Your data goes only to that machine. Whoever operates it holds it and can read it. The third parties listed further down are still contacted by the app itself.

Family Connect is not end-to-end encrypted

This matters enough to say plainly and early. Messages, attachments, board notes and shared locations are stored in plaintext in the server's PostgreSQL database and on its disk. The server reads message text in order to build notification text, chat-list previews and reply quotations.

Traffic between the app and the server is encrypted in transit by TLS (plain http is accepted only for a server on your own network), and the audio and video of a call are encrypted peer-to-peer (DTLS-SRTP) so that a relay cannot decode them. But the operator of the server you use can read your messages. If that operator is me, I can. Please do not treat Family Connect as a secret-keeping tool; treat it as your household's own message board that happens to live on a computer somebody in your family trusts.

What the app sends to the server

DataNotes
Account details Username, display name and password. No e-mail address and no phone number are collected. The password is stored on the server only as an Argon2id hash, never in readable form.
Birthday (optional) A day and a month only — never a year. Your age cannot be derived from it. A family owner can set or clear any member's birthday.
Profile picture (optional) Re-drawn on your PC as a square JPEG of at most 512 px before upload, so the original photo's EXIF metadata (including any GPS tag) is not sent. Visible to your family and to nobody else.
Message text Everything you type, including in one-to-one chats.
Photos Re-drawn on your PC as a JPEG of at most 2048 px before upload, which removes EXIF metadata including any GPS coordinates. The exception is an animated format — GIF and WebP — which a JPEG cannot hold: those go as files, with their own bytes and whatever metadata they carry.
Videos and files Uploaded byte for byte, unmodified. Any metadata the file already contains travels with it — a phone video typically carries the location it was recorded at, the device model and the date; a document typically carries its author and revision history. Family Connect does not strip these. This includes files you hand to the app through the Windows Share menu, paste or drag in.
Voice notes Recorded in the app from your microphone and uploaded as audio files.
Shared locations Full-precision WGS-84 latitude and longitude plus an accuracy radius. These are not rounded, blurred or reduced to a neighbourhood. Sharing a location is always an explicit action, and it is one position, taken once — the app runs no location service in the background.
Board notes, polls and reactions Text, colour, position and author. Poll votes are attributed and visible to everyone in the chat — a poll is not a secret ballot.
Read receipts Your last-read position in each chat, with a timestamp, shared with the family. There is no setting to turn this off.
Notification tokens None, on Windows. The Windows app registers no device with any push service. It raises its notifications itself, from its own connection to your server, while it is running — see Notifications below.

Family statistics are visible to every member of your family, not only the owner: how many messages each person sent, how many photos, videos, voice notes and locations, the total size, and how many assistant questions they asked.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, no advertising SDK, no attribution SDK, no crash-reporting service of ours, no third-party tracker of any kind.
  • No access to your contacts, your photo library or your files beyond the ones you pick, paste, drop or share into a chat.
  • No advertising identifier, no fingerprinting, no cross-app tracking.
  • No camera use inside a chat: the camera is used for video calls only.
  • The server records no IP addresses, user agents or device fingerprints in its database. (A web server in front of it, such as nginx, keeps its own access log by default; that is the operator's to configure.)

Notifications

Windows is told about a new message by the app itself, from its live connection to your server, and only while Family Connect is running — in its window, or in the notification area if Keep running when the window is closed is on. No push service is involved, so no message of yours passes through Microsoft to reach this PC. A notification says who wrote, never what they wrote, and Tell me when a message arrives in Settings turns them off.

The other members of your family may use Family Connect on phones and Macs. What you send from Windows reaches their notifications through Apple (APNs) or Google (FCM), and by default that payload includes the full message text, your display name and the family's name. A server operator can turn the message text off; the names still travel.

Other companies your PC or your server contacts

Even on a server you run yourself, some things necessarily leave your network. Each is named here, and the ones that can be switched off are switched off in the app's Settings.

WhoWhat they receive, and when
OpenStreetMap (map tiles) When a shared location is displayed, your PC asks OpenStreetMap's tile servers for the squares of map around that place, which tells OpenStreetMap roughly where the place is and shows it your IP address. Requests carry no cookies and no referrer; each tile is kept on your PC for a month so it is not asked for again. Turn Map Previews off in Settings and a shared location shows as a pin and a label, with no tile request. Clicking Open in Maps opens the place in your web browser on Apple Maps' website, which that site then receives — only when you click.
Any website linked in a message To draw a link preview, your PC fetches the page directly from that site, which sees a request from your IP address. The request is https only, carries no cookies, credentials or referrer, and stops reading at the end of the page's header or after 1 MB. Turn Link Previews off in Settings to stop it. When previews are on, the request is made for every message carrying a link, including messages hidden because you blocked their sender — deliberately, so that blocking somebody cannot be detected by the owner of a linked site.
Google (public STUN server), or whichever server your operator names A call asks the addresses your server hands out — by default stun.l.google.com — to discover your public address, which reveals your IP address to Google. An operator can point this at their own server instead, and can run a relay for networks that block direct connections. A successful call also necessarily reveals each participant's IP address to the other participant; that is inherent to peer-to-peer calling and no setting can remove it while calls still work.
Microsoft (Azure OpenAI) — only if the server operator has enabled the assistant The assistant is off unless an operator configures it. Where it is enabled, mentioning @ai in the family chat sends recent family conversation — up to 200 messages or 30 days, including other members' words, their display names and timestamps — to Azure OpenAI to answer the question. A family owner can switch that history off, leaving only the message that mentioned the assistant. Private chats with the assistant send only that conversation. This is sent by the server, not by your PC.

What Microsoft and Windows handle around the app

Family Connect itself sends nothing to Microsoft. A few Windows components it relies on have their own practices, governed by Microsoft's privacy statement rather than by this page:

ComponentWhat it does, and what Family Connect does about it
WebView2 Runtime (calls) The audio and video of a call run in Microsoft's Chromium-based WebView2 component, which ships with Windows 11. It is started only the first time you place or answer a call, it only ever shows a page bundled inside the app, and the microphone and camera are granted to that page alone. The runtime collects diagnostic data about its own health and crashes according to your Windows Settings ▸ Privacy & security ▸ Diagnostics & feedback setting. It also includes Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, and Microsoft requires this notice: the software includes Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, which collects and sends information to Microsoft as described in Microsoft's privacy statement (SmartScreen). Family Connect adds nothing to this stream and reads nothing from it.
Windows location When you choose to share where you are, the app asks Windows for one position. Windows decides how to find it (satellite, Wi-Fi or network), under your Settings ▸ Privacy & security ▸ Location choices, which can refuse the app at any time.
Windows notifications Shown by Windows on this PC, from the app, with no push service in between. Settings ▸ System ▸ Notifications can silence them.
Microsoft Store Because the app is distributed through the Store, Microsoft reports acquisition counts, ratings and aggregated crash and hang counts — which Windows itself collects under the same diagnostic-data setting — to us as numbers on a publisher dashboard. Nothing in them identifies anybody, and the app sends nothing in order to produce them.

What the app keeps on your PC

Everything below stays on the PC, in the app's own local folder (or, for the session token, in Windows' own credential store). None of it is sent anywhere by the app, and removing the app removes its folder.

StoredWhy
cache.db — your chats, messages, the family board, the roster, and any message you wrote that the server has not received yet So history reads back with no network, and a message typed offline is sent when the connection returns. Signing out empties it, unsent messages included — a queued message belongs to the account that wrote it.
Downloaded photos, videos, voice notes, files and profile pictures (blobs) So an attachment is fetched once rather than every time it is shown.
Files on their way out (outgoing) Copies of what you attached, kept only while the send is under way, so an interrupted upload can finish.
Map tiles from OpenStreetMap Kept for a month, so a map already drawn is not asked for again.
The session token, in the Windows Credential Locker, one per server So you stay signed in. It is the key to your account, so it lives in Windows' protected credential store and never in a file; signing out removes it. Your password is not stored.
Small settings files: the server address, Tell me when a message arrives, Keep running when the window is closed, Link Previews, Map Previews, and the window's position So the app opens the way you left it.
awaiting-join.txt — the server and your account number, only while a request to join a family is waiting for the owner's approval So the app can tell you if the owner declined.
diagnostics.log — a line per failure: a crash, an error, a connection problem So a failure you can see has something behind it. It never contains the text of a message or a token — it records what happened, not what was said. It is written for you, on your PC, and there is no crash report of ours to send it to; you can read or delete it at any time, and attach it to a bug report if you choose.
The WebView2 data folder, once you have made a call The call's browser engine needs somewhere to keep its own state.
Temporary copies in the app's temporary folder A picture you paste, and a file you pass to the Windows Share menu, are written there first. Windows may clear it at any time.

Anyone who can sign in to your Windows account can open the app and read what it holds, as with any messenger on a shared PC. Signing out of Family Connect clears its history from this PC.

How long things are kept

The server deletes messages older than a limit its operator sets — 100 days by default. Deleting a message also deletes the photos, videos, voice notes and files it carried. An operator can lengthen this, shorten it, or switch it off entirely, so on a server you do not run yourself you should ask its operator. A copy already downloaded to your PC stays in the app's cache until you sign out or remove the app.

The sweep is not a general erasure. It does not touch:

  • board notes,
  • profile pictures,
  • accounts, sign-in sessions or registered devices,
  • blocks,
  • and, most importantly, moderation reports and their quoted text — see the next section.

One further caveat: if two people send the identical file, the server stores one copy and both messages point at it. Deleting or expiring one of those messages does not remove the file while the other still refers to it.

Reporting a message, and what it makes permanent

Any member can report a message or a person to the family's owner. When a message is reported, a complete copy of its text is frozen and stored for the owner to read. That copy:

  • survives the author editing the message afterwards,
  • survives the retention sweep deleting the original,
  • survives the report being marked as handled,
  • and survives the author deleting their account.

This includes messages from one-to-one chats, and it is the only route by which a private message in Family Connect reaches a third person. It requires the deliberate act of the person who received it, and the app says so before you confirm. A report about the family owner is never shown to that owner; if the problem is the owner, the server operator's published contact is shown on the same screen as an escalation route.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app: Settings ▸ Delete Account…. Be clear about what this does:

  • It removes your identity. Username, password, profile picture, birthday, sessions and registered devices are erased, and every device you were signed in on is signed out.
  • It does not remove what you wrote. Your messages in the family chat, your board notes, your emoji reactions and your poll votes remain, re-labelled as coming from a “Deleted account”. This is deliberate: the rest of your family did not agree to lose their conversation.
  • It does delete your one-to-one chats in full — including the other person's messages in them. That person is not asked.

If you want your messages gone as well, delete them or ask the operator of your server; on the default server, write to me and I will do it.

Children

Family Connect is built for households, and children are an expected part of that. It collects no age and no date of birth, so it cannot and does not profile anyone by age. There is no advertising, no tracking and no public directory — a Family Connect account can only be reached by people who are in the same family, and joining a family needs an invite code and, by default, the owner's approval. A parent or guardian who sets up the family is the person who decides who may join it, and who can remove a member at any time.

Your rights

If you use the default server, write to nettrash@nettrash.me and I will tell you what is stored about you, correct it, export it or delete it. There is one honest limit: I cannot remove your messages from other people's conversations without removing part of theirs, so I will discuss what you want before doing anything irreversible.

If you use somebody else's server, I hold nothing about you and cannot act on it; your request goes to whoever runs that server. What the app keeps on your own PC is yours to delete: sign out, or remove the app.

Where data is held

The default server runs on a machine in the European Union. Notifications to other members' phones and Macs pass through Apple and Google, map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, and the assistant, where enabled, runs through Microsoft — these organisations operate globally and may process the data described above outside your country. A self-hosted server is wherever you put it.
