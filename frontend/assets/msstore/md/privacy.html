Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 September 2026 · Applies to: md 1.0 for Windows — the Markdown editor published by nettrash on the Microsoft Store, for Windows 11. This policy is versioned alongside the app's source code; the most recent commit on main is authoritative.

TL;DR

md does not collect, transmit, sell, or share any data. It contains no analytics, no advertising SDKs, no trackers, and no servers operated by us — we operate no servers at all. The documents you open and edit stay where you put them — on your PC, in File Explorer, or in your own cloud-synced folders. The app's only network activity is fetching images that your own documents reference by a remote URL, so they can appear in the preview and in what you print or export. Nothing about you is ever sent anywhere.

If that already answers your question, you don't need to read the rest.

What we collect

Nothing. md has no account to create, no email to register, and no telemetry pinging home. There are no servers in this picture. The app initiates exactly one kind of network request, and only on your behalf: downloading an image that your document points at by remote URL (![…](https://…)). See Network use below.

Two things around the app are Microsoft's, and this page would be dishonest if it let the paragraph above stand for them: the WebView2 Runtime that draws the preview, and the Microsoft Store that distributes the app. Both are described in What Microsoft collects around the app below. md adds nothing to either stream and reads nothing from it.

Network use

One and only one thing in md causes a network request: an image your own document references by a remote URL. It is fetched when that document is rendered — in the live preview, and also when you print it or export it to PDF or EPUB, because the printed page has to show the same picture the preview does.

The request goes directly to whatever host your document names. That host sees your PC's IP address and a normal HTTP request, exactly as it would if you had opened the same URL in a web browser — that is unavoidable for any app that displays a remote image, and it is the only reason md touches the network. We are not party to it: no request is ever made to us, because there is nothing of ours to make it to. If your document references no remote images, md makes no network requests at all. A local image beside the document is deliberately not shown in the preview; the one place md reads it is when you export a TextBundle, where it is copied into the bundle's assets folder — from disk, with no network involved.

There is no telemetry, no analytics, no crash reporting of ours, no ads, and no tracking of any kind.

Your documents

md is a document editor. The files you open, create and save are handled through the standard Windows file dialogs, file associations and drag and drop, and are stored wherever you choose — locally on your PC, or in a folder that your own cloud provider syncs. We never see them.

If you keep a document in a synced folder, it syncs through your account under that provider's privacy terms, not ours. We have no access to your documents under any circumstance.

Documents saved in a legacy text encoding (UTF-16 with a byte-order mark, or Windows-1251) are decoded on your PC when you open them and written back the same way. No part of that happens off the PC.

Writer mode and books

In writer mode a book is simply a folder you pick on your own storage: its subfolders are chapters, the Markdown files inside them are articles, and both are ordered by name. Creating, renaming, reordering and deleting chapters and articles are ordinary file operations in that folder — reordering writes the new order back into the file names. Compiling the book into a single PDF, or exporting it as an EPUB 3 or LaTeX file, happens entirely on your PC and writes only to the destination you choose.

md never uploads a book, indexes it, or copies it anywhere. It only touches the folder you handed it, and only while that book is open.

The example documents — and the sample book — are bundled inside the app. Opening one downloads nothing; unpacking the sample book writes it to a folder you choose.

Private author notes. An HTML comment placed on its own line whose text begins with note: — for example <!-- note: fix this later --> — is treated as your private note: it is listed for you in the Go menu but is kept out of the preview, out of print, and out of every PDF and EPUB you export. This applies only to comments that start their own line; a comment written inline in the middle of a line is left alone and renders as part of your text, so don't rely on an inline comment to hide anything.

Printing, PDF and EPUB

Print, Share Rendered PDF, Export as PDF and Export as EPUB all run on your PC, inside the WebView2 renderer, and the PDF exports produce real pages — A4 by default, or A5, US Letter, US Legal or a paperback trim size you choose. Pagination is line-aware, so no line of text is sliced in half at a page boundary; a diagram taller than a single page can still be split across pages. A line containing only \newpage (or \pagebreak) starts a fresh page.

Printed and exported pages are always white with dark ink, whichever theme you use in the app — the theme affects the screen, never the page. The resulting file goes only where you send it: the destination you pick, or the Windows Share pane's target you choose.

Third-party code

md's .NET side is dependency-light — nothing beyond Microsoft's own Windows App SDK and Windows SDK build tools; no dependency manager pulls in an analytics or advertising SDK, and nothing phones home.

To render math and diagrams, the app bundles a small set of open-source JavaScript engines inside the package itself: KaTeX with its mhchem chemistry extension (math), Mermaid (diagrams), Viz.js / Graphviz, PlantUML and highlight.js (code). They run on your PC, offline, inside the WebView2 view that draws the preview and the printed page. They ship in the package: nothing is downloaded at runtime, no CDN is contacted, and your document's contents are never sent to any service to be rendered.

The WebView2 Runtime itself is Microsoft's — the Chromium-based component that ships with Windows 11 and is updated by Microsoft — and is covered below.

What the app stores on your PC

md keeps a few small settings in the package's own settings store, and a few files in the package's own folders. All of it is local, none of it is personal, and none of it is transmitted anywhere. If a future version remembers anything else, it is added to this list in the same commit that adds it:

Stored Why
Your view layout (Edit / Split / Preview) for each of the 200 most recently opened documents — as a short hash of the file's path, not the path itself (md.viewModeMemory) So each document opens the way you left it.
The PDF page size you chose (md.pdfPageSize) So the next export uses it without asking.
The path of the book folder you picked (md.bookBookmark), plus Windows' own future-access grant for it So writer mode can reopen your book without asking you to find the folder again. It identifies a location you chose; Close Book removes both.
Which article was open last (md.bookLastArticle), the book window's layout (md.bookViewMode) and whether articles open in separate windows (md.bookOpensInSeparateWindows) So the book reopens on the article you were writing, the way you were writing it.
The last size of a document window and of the book window (md.win.windowSize.document, md.win.windowSize.book) So new windows open at the size you last used.
session.json — the saved documents you had open when md last closed, each with its layout, Zen state and window position, and whether the book window was open and where So a plain launch reopens them. Untitled drafts are not in it; it holds file paths and window geometry, nothing else.
The WebView2 user-data folder (the preview's cache and state, including your last print-dialog choices), under the package's local cache The Chromium renderer needs somewhere to keep its state. It can hold only what the preview loaded, which is md's own page.
Temporary files — the PDF or Markdown copy handed to the Windows Share pane, and the PDF an export renders before it is copied to the file you named Written to the package's temporary folder, which holds nothing but those copies. Windows may clear it at any time, and removing the app removes it.
md.log — an append-only error log beside session.json, written only when something goes wrong: an unhandled exception, a file md was asked to open that it could not, or a start-up on which Windows gave md no package identity (settings are not saved that run) So a failure you can see has something behind it. A line is a timestamp and the error's own text, which can name the file md was reading or writing. It is written for you, on your PC: nothing is sent anywhere, there is no crash report of ours to send it to, and you can read or delete the file at any time.
Windows' own recent-items list, which File ▸ Open Recent and the taskbar Jump List show Maintained by Windows, not by md; Clear Menu empties md's list.
Only if a save fails while a window closes: a copy of your text as <name> (rescued) with the original extension (Notes (rescued).md), beside your document So a full disk or a locked file never costs you a paragraph. It is your file, in your folder; md never touches it again.

Permissions

md asks for no camera, microphone, contacts, location, or photo-library access, and shows no tracking prompt. It does use the network — but only for the remote images described above. Its package declares exactly one capability:

Capability What it means
runFullTrust md is a classic Windows desktop app: it runs with your own user's rights, the way a program installed from any other source does, rather than inside an app container. That is worth saying plainly, because it is not a sandbox. md reads and writes only the files you open, save, drop on it or pick as a book — by how it is written, which anyone can read in its public source, not by a fence the operating system draws around it.
(no network capability) A full-trust app needs none to reach the network, so none is declared — please do not read that as "no network". The one request md makes is the remote-image fetch described under Network use; the WebView2 Runtime makes it, to the host your own document names.

What Microsoft collects around the app

md itself collects nothing. Two Microsoft components it depends on have their own practices, governed by Microsoft's privacy statement rather than by this page:

Component What Microsoft collects, and what md does about it
WebView2 Runtime The runtime collects diagnostic data about its own health, crashes and API usage. Microsoft documents that this follows your Windows Settings ▸ Privacy & security ▸ Diagnostics & feedback ▸ Diagnostic data setting, that a required minimum is collected regardless of it, and that a crash inside the runtime sends a crash dump to Microsoft. The runtime also includes Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, which md leaves enabled, and Microsoft requires every such app to give this notice: the software includes Microsoft Defender SmartScreen, which collects and sends information to Microsoft as described in Microsoft's privacy statement (SmartScreen). In md's case the preview only ever loads the page md builds on your PC — a link you click opens in your browser, not in the preview — so there is little for SmartScreen to look at, but the component is there and the notice stands. md adds nothing to this stream and reads nothing from it.
Microsoft Store Because the app is distributed through the Store, Microsoft reports acquisition counts, ratings and — for a packaged app — aggregated crash and hang counts that Windows itself collects under the same diagnostic-data setting, to us as numbers on a publisher dashboard. Nothing in them identifies anybody, and the app sends nothing in order to produce them.

Children's privacy

Because md collects no data at all, it collects no data from children.
