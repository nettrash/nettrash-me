Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026 · Applies to: Family Connect 1.3 for Linux — the family messenger published by nettrash for Ubuntu 26.04 LTS, Fedora 44 and later, and other distributions through a portable archive (x86-64 and ARM64). The same service is described for the iPhone, iPad, Mac and Android apps on their own page and for Windows on another; what happens on the server is identical, and this page adds what is particular to Linux.

This policy is versioned alongside the app's source code; the most recent commit on master of github.com/nettrash/family.connect is authoritative.

Read this part first: there are two different situations

Family Connect is self-hosted software. Your family's messages live on one server, and who runs that server decides who can read them. The Linux app has no server built in: the first thing it asks for is an address. There are two cases, and they are genuinely different:

If you… Then…
entered https://fc.nettrash.me Your app is connected to a server I operate. I hold your messages, photos, videos, voice and video messages, files, shared locations, profile picture and account details, and I am technically able to read all of them. For you, I am the data controller.
entered the address of your own server, or of a server somebody else runs I receive nothing at all. Your data goes only to that machine. Whoever operates it holds it and can read it. The third parties listed further down are still contacted by the app itself.

Family Connect is not end-to-end encrypted

This matters enough to say plainly and early. A Family Connect 1.3 server seals what people write, say and show — messages, attachments, board notes, shared locations — with its own key before it reaches its database or its disk, so a backup or a copy of that disk opens nothing without the key. But the server holds the key: it opens what it stores in order to deliver it, and it reads message text to build notification text, chat-list previews and reply quotations.

Traffic between the app and the server is encrypted in transit by TLS (plain http is accepted only for a server on your own network, and the app warns when an address uses it), and the audio and video of a call are encrypted peer-to-peer (DTLS-SRTP) so that a relay cannot decode them. But the operator of the server you use can read your messages. If that operator is me, I can. Please do not treat Family Connect as a secret-keeping tool; treat it as your household's own message board that happens to live on a computer somebody in your family trusts.

What the app sends to the server

DataNotes
Account details Username, display name and password. No e-mail address and no phone number are collected. The password is stored on the server only as an Argon2id hash, never in readable form.
Birthday (optional) A day and a month only — never a year. Your age cannot be derived from it. A family owner can set or clear any member's birthday.
Profile picture (optional) Fitted on your computer to at most 512 px and written as a JPEG, which leaves the original's metadata behind — except that a JPEG or PNG already that small is sent as it is, with whatever metadata it carries. Visible to your family and to nobody else.
Message text Everything you type, including in one-to-one chats and groups.
Photos Re-drawn on your computer as a JPEG of at most 2048 px before upload, which removes their metadata, including any GPS coordinates. An animated GIF or WebP is made into a short silent looping video where the app can, and otherwise goes as the file it is.
Videos, sound and files A video or a sound file is made smaller on your computer before it goes. Any other file is uploaded byte for byte, unmodified, and any metadata it already contains travels with it — a document typically carries its author and revision history. Family Connect does not strip these. This includes files you drag in, paste, or open with Family Connect from your file manager.
Voice and video messages Recorded in the app from your microphone and camera, and uploaded as files.
Shared locations Full-precision WGS-84 latitude and longitude plus an accuracy radius. These are not rounded, blurred or reduced to a neighbourhood. Sharing a location is always an explicit action, and it is one position, taken once — the app runs no location service in the background.
Board notes, polls and reactions Text, colour, position and author. Poll votes are attributed and visible to everyone in the chat — a poll is not a secret ballot.
Read receipts Your last-read position in each chat, with a timestamp, shared with the family. There is no setting to turn this off.
Notification tokens None, on Linux. The Linux app registers no device with any push service. It raises its notifications itself, from its own connection to your server, while it is running — see Notifications below.

Family statistics are visible to every member of your family, not only the owner: how many messages each person sent, how many photos, videos, voice notes and locations, the total size, how many assistant questions they asked, and how many web searches the assistant made to answer them.

Family Hosting. The Linux app sells nothing and handles no payment. If you bought a Family Hosting subscription in the iPhone, iPad or Mac app, Apple handled the payment, and the server keeps the subscription's identifiers, product and end date — never a card, a name or an Apple ID — so that it knows your family is paid for.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, no advertising SDK, no attribution SDK, no crash-reporting service, no update check, no third-party tracker of any kind.
  • No access to your contacts or your files beyond the ones you pick, paste, drop or open with it.
  • No advertising identifier, no fingerprinting, no cross-app tracking.
  • The camera is used for video calls and for recording a video message, and nowhere else.
  • The server records no IP addresses, user agents or device fingerprints in its database. (A web server in front of it, such as nginx, keeps its own access log by default; that is the operator's to configure.)

Notifications

Your desktop is told about a new message by the app itself, from its live connection to your server, and only while Family Connect is running — in its window, or behind its icon in the top bar if Keep running in the top bar is on. No push service is involved, so no message of yours passes through anybody else to reach this computer. A notification says who wrote and where, never what they wrote, and Tell me when a message arrives in Settings turns them off.

The other members of your family may use Family Connect on phones and Macs. What you send from Linux reaches their notifications through Apple (APNs) or Google (FCM), and by default that payload includes the full message text, your display name and the family's name. A server operator can turn the message text off; the names still travel.

Other companies your computer or your server contacts

Even on a server you run yourself, some things necessarily leave your network. Each is named here, and the ones that can be switched off are switched off in the app's Settings, under Privacy.

WhoWhat they receive, and when
OpenStreetMap (map tiles) When a shared location is displayed, your computer asks OpenStreetMap's tile servers for the squares of map around that place, which tells OpenStreetMap roughly where the place is and shows it your IP address. Requests carry no cookies and no referrer; each tile is kept on your computer for a month so it is not asked for again. Turn Map Previews off and a shared location shows its pin with no tile request. Clicking a place opens it on openstreetmap.org in your web browser — only when you click.
Any website linked in a message To draw a link preview, your computer fetches the page directly from that site, which sees a request from your IP address. The request is https only, carries no cookies, credentials or referrer, and stops reading at the end of the page's header or after 1 MB. Turn Link Previews off to stop it.
Google (public STUN server), or whichever server your operator names A call asks the addresses your server hands out — by default stun.l.google.com — to discover your public address, which reveals your IP address to Google. An operator can point this at their own server instead, and can run a relay for networks that block direct connections. A successful call also necessarily reveals each participant's IP address to the other participant; that is inherent to peer-to-peer calling and no setting can remove it while calls still work.
Microsoft (Azure OpenAI) — only if the server operator has enabled the assistant The assistant is off unless an operator configures it. Where it is enabled, mentioning @ai in the family chat or a group sends recent conversation there — up to 200 messages or 30 days, including other members' words, their display names and timestamps — to Azure OpenAI to answer the question. A family owner can switch that history off, leaving only the message that mentioned the assistant. Private chats with the assistant send only that conversation. Where the operator allows it, Show text under a voice message or a video sends that one recording's sound to the same service, to write it out.

Nothing is sent until you have agreed to it. Before your first message can reach the assistant, the app shows you a screen naming the service that will receive it and everything that travels with it, and nothing is sent unless you agree there. The server itself refuses to call the model for anybody who has not, so a client cannot bypass it. You can stop it at any time in Settings → Assistant; that takes effect immediately, although what has already been sent cannot be recalled. The history a mention carries is filtered by each sender's own agreement, so declining also keeps your words out of somebody else's question, and a recording you sent is written out only with your agreement. All of this is sent by the server, not by your computer.
A web-search service (Brave Search, or a SearXNG instance), Open-Meteo (weather) and Wikipedia — only if the server operator has set them up, the family owner has turned lookups on, and you have agreed The assistant can look something up when a question needs it. This is off unless three people each say yes: the server operator chooses which of these services exist (none, by default), the family owner turns lookups on for the family, and you agree to it separately, in Settings or on the screen that first asks. What leaves is small, and it is sent by the server, not by your computer: a short search phrase the assistant writes from the question, or for the weather a place name. Nothing else goes with it: not the conversation, not anybody's name or account, no photos, and never your location or coordinates.

What your desktop handles around the app

Family Connect uses the parts of your Linux desktop that every desktop app uses. They belong to your distribution, not to Family Connect, and send nothing of the app's anywhere:

ComponentWhat it does
Keyring (the Secret Service: GNOME Keyring, or your desktop's own) Keeps your sign-in, one per server, under the label “Family Connect (<server>)”. Without a keyring the app keeps it in memory only and you sign in at every start; it never writes it to a file.
Desktop portals (camera and location) The camera is asked for through the desktop's Camera portal, and a location through its Location portal — your desktop asks you, remembers the answer, and can take it back in its privacy settings. The location service (GeoClue) decides how to find you, which may include asking a Wi-Fi location service your distribution configures.
PipeWire (microphone) The microphone is your desktop's default input, used only while you record or are on a call.
Notifications Shown by your desktop on this computer, from the app, with no push service in between. Your desktop's notification settings can silence them.
WebKitGTK (formulas, diagrams and graphs) Draws them on your computer from engines bundled in the app, in a page that can reach no network, keeps no storage and is granted no permission. On Ubuntu, the .deb's AppArmor profile lets WebKit start its own sandbox and grants nothing else.
journald (the app's log) A line per failure, readable with journalctl --user -t family-connect. It never contains the text of a message, a name, a token or a link; it stays on your computer, and there is no crash report of ours to send it to.

What the app keeps on your computer

Everything below stays on the computer, in your home folder (or, for the sign-in, in your keyring). None of it is sent anywhere by the app.

StoredWhy
~/.local/state/family-connect/cache.db — your chats, messages, the family board, the roster, and any message you wrote that the server has not received yet; with the attachments on their way out and any recording you did not send, beside it So history reads back with no network, and a message typed offline is sent when the connection returns. Logging out, being removed from the family, deleting your account or changing server empties it.
~/.cache/family-connect — downloaded photos, videos, voice notes, files and profile pictures, drawn formulas and diagrams, and map tiles So an attachment is fetched once rather than every time it is shown. Everything here can be rebuilt; map tiles are kept for a month.
~/.local/share/family-connect/inbox Files you opened with Family Connect from another app, until they are sent.
~/.config/family-connect/settings.conf — the server address and the switches in Settings So the app opens the way you left it.
~/.config/autostart/me.nettrash.FamilyConnect.desktop, only while Start when I log in is on So your desktop starts the app, hidden in the top bar, as you log in.

Anyone who can log in to your desktop account can open the app and read what it holds, as with any messenger on a shared computer. Removing the package leaves these files, as removing a package does on Linux; log out of Family Connect first, then delete the folders above to remove them.

How long things are kept

The server deletes messages older than a limit its operator sets — 100 days by default. Deleting a message also deletes the photos, videos, voice notes and files it carried. An operator can lengthen this, shorten it, or switch it off entirely, so on a server you do not run yourself you should ask its operator. A copy already downloaded to your computer stays in the app's cache until you log out or delete it.

The sweep is not a general erasure. It does not touch:

  • board notes,
  • profile pictures,
  • accounts, sign-in sessions or registered devices,
  • blocks,
  • and, most importantly, moderation reports and their quoted text — see the next section.

One further caveat: if two people send the identical file, the server stores one copy and both messages point at it. Deleting or expiring one of those messages does not remove the file while the other still refers to it.

Reporting a message, and what it makes permanent

Any member can report a message or a person to the family's owner. When a message is reported, a complete copy of its text is frozen and stored for the owner to read. That copy survives the author editing the message afterwards, the retention sweep deleting the original, the report being marked as handled, and the author deleting their account.

This includes messages from one-to-one chats, and it is the only route by which a private message in Family Connect reaches a third person. It requires the deliberate act of the person who received it, and the app says so before you confirm. A report about the family owner is never shown to that owner. A report about something the assistant wrote goes to whoever runs the server, not to the family owner.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account in the app: Settings… ▸ Server ▸ Delete Account…. Be clear about what this does:

  • It removes your identity. Username, password, profile picture, birthday, sessions and registered devices are erased, and every device you were signed in on is signed out.
  • It does not remove what you wrote. Your messages in the family chat and in groups, your board notes, your emoji reactions and your poll votes remain, re-labelled as coming from a “Deleted account”. This is deliberate: the rest of your family did not agree to lose their conversation.
  • It does delete your one-to-one chats in full — including the other person's messages in them. That person is not asked.

If you want your messages gone as well, delete them or ask the operator of your server; on fc.nettrash.me, write to me and I will do it.

Children

Family Connect is built for households, and children are an expected part of that. It collects no age and no date of birth, so it cannot and does not profile anyone by age. There is no advertising, no tracking and no public directory — a Family Connect account can only be reached by people who are in the same family, and joining a family needs an invite code and, by default, the owner's approval. A parent or guardian who sets up the family is the person who decides who may join it, and who can remove a member at any time.

Your rights

If you use fc.nettrash.me, write to nettrash@nettrash.me and I will tell you what is stored about you, correct it, export it or delete it. There is one honest limit: I cannot remove your messages from other people's conversations without removing part of theirs, so I will discuss what you want before doing anything irreversible.

If you use somebody else's server, I hold nothing about you and cannot act on it; your request goes to whoever runs that server. What the app keeps on your own computer is yours to delete: log out, or delete the folders listed above.

The protection third parties give

Where data leaves the server it leaves for one of the companies named above. For the optional assistant, Microsoft's Azure OpenAI Service terms provide that prompts and responses are not used to train or improve Microsoft's or any third party's models and are not shared with other customers or with OpenAI. The assistant's lookup services — Brave, Open-Meteo and the Wikimedia Foundation — handle the requests they receive under their own published privacy policies, and a SearXNG instance is run by the operator; what they receive is kept small for that reason. OpenStreetMap and a linked website receive only the requests described above, under their own policies. No data described here is sold, rented, or handed to a data broker, and nothing is used for advertising or profiling.

Where data is held

fc.nettrash.me runs on a machine in the European Union. Notifications to other members' phones and Macs pass through Apple and Google, map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, the assistant, where enabled, runs through Microsoft, and its lookups, where turned on, go to the web-search service the operator chose, Open-Meteo and the Wikimedia Foundation — these organisations operate globally and may process the data described above outside your country. A self-hosted server is wherever you put it.
