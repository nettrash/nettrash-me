Privacy Policy

Effective date: 14 July 2026 · Applies to: md 1.2 — the Markdown editor published by nettrash on Google Play (me.nettrash.md), and the same upload-key-signed APK side-loaded from nettrash.me/play/md/md-latest.apk.

This policy is versioned alongside the app's source code; the most recent commit on master is authoritative.

TL;DR

md collects no data at all — nothing is transmitted to us, sold, or shared. There are no analytics, no advertising SDKs, no crash reporters, no third-party trackers, and no servers operated by us. The documents and books you open and edit stay where you put them — on your device or in whatever storage location you pick.

Two things are worth knowing precisely, because a blanket "no network, no libraries" claim would be untrue:

  • md makes exactly one kind of network request: fetching an image that your own document references by a remote URL. It happens in the preview and when printing or exporting to PDF or EPUB. The request goes to whatever host your document names, and that host sees your device's IP address — exactly as it would if you opened the same image in a browser. Nothing else is ever sent anywhere.
  • md bundles several open-source JavaScript engines (KaTeX, Mermaid, Viz.js/Graphviz, PlantUML) to render maths and diagrams. They run on your device, offline, inside the preview's web view; they ship inside the app and nothing about them is downloaded at runtime.

If that already answers your question, you don't need to read the rest.

What we collect

Nothing. md has no account to create, no email to register, and no telemetry pinging home. There are no servers in this picture: md never contacts a service of ours, because none exists.

md declares the INTERNET permission for exactly one purpose: downloading images that a document references by remote URL (![…](https://…)), so that they can be displayed in the preview and included when you print or export to PDF or EPUB. Those requests go directly to whatever host the document names, carry no identifying data beyond an ordinary HTTP request, and happen only for documents that reference such images. A document with no remote images produces no network traffic at all. If the URL matters to you, remember that the host you (or the document's author) chose can see your IP address; use local image files if you'd rather it couldn't.

Your documents

md is a document editor. The files you open, create and save are handled entirely by Android's Storage Access Framework (the system document architecture): you pick each file or folder through the system document picker, and md is granted access only to what you explicitly choose. Your documents are stored wherever you put them — in on-device storage, or in whatever provider you select in the picker (Google Drive, your device's Downloads, an SD card, and so on). We never see them.

If you store a document in a cloud provider through the picker, it syncs through your account with that provider, under their privacy terms, not ours. We have no access to your documents under any circumstance.

Writer mode and books

A book in md is simply a folder you pick with the system folder picker: its subfolders are chapters, its Markdown files are articles, and both are ordered by name. Creating, renaming, reordering and deleting chapters and articles are ordinary file operations — reordering writes the new order back into the file names — performed on the folder you granted access to, and nowhere else. Compiling a book into one PDF, or exporting it as an EPUB 3 file, happens entirely on your device; the result is written to the destination you choose in the picker.

The same is true of every other authoring feature: the table of contents, live word and character counts, synchronized split scrolling, private author notes, and the bundled examples (including the sample book) are all computed locally from the text in front of you. Private author notes — an HTML comment on its own line whose text begins with note:, i.e. <!-- note: … --> — are exactly as private as the file that holds them: md keeps them out of the rendered preview, print, PDF and EPUB output, but they remain plain text inside your own document, so anyone you send that .md file to can read them. (An HTML comment written inline, in the middle of a line, is not treated as a note: it stays in the text and appears, verbatim, in the rendered page and the PDF.)

Data stored on your device

md stores a small amount of app-private state — all of it local, none of it personal, none of it transmitted:

  • The view layout (Edit / Split / Preview), held in the app's saved instance state so the editor survives a rotation or a fold. It is not written to disk, and a cold start opens on the default layout.
  • A pointer to the book folder you picked, if you use writer mode: the persisted Storage Access Framework tree URI (and, for a book created with New Book…, the name of the subfolder inside it), kept in the app's SharedPreferences. It is a reference to a location you chose, not a copy of your content, and closing the book discards it.

There is no account, no analytics store, no usage history, and no cache of your documents' content. Your documents themselves live in the locations you chose through the picker, not inside the app.

The app manifest leaves Android's Auto Backup at its default (allowBackup="true"). The only app-private data it could capture is the handful of preferences listed above; whatever the system backs up goes to your own Google account, encrypted, and never to us.

Permissions md asks for

One. md declares a single Android permission:

Permission Requested?
INTERNET / network access Yes — solely so that images your documents reference by remote URL can be fetched, both for the preview and for printing / PDF / EPUB export. The app calls no services of ours (there are none) and uploads nothing. The page being rendered is local HTML md builds itself from your text; the only thing it may load from the network is those images.
Storage (READ_/WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE, READ_MEDIA_*) No. File access is mediated entirely by the Storage Access Framework, which grants access to the specific files you pick without any storage permission.
Camera, microphone, contacts, location, calendar No — never requested.

Documents and books are opened four ways, all of them system-mediated: the in-app Open / Save pickers (OpenDocument / CreateDocument, with the access you grant persisted so md can reopen that file); the folder picker used by Open Book… / New Book… (OpenDocumentTree, which grants md the folder you chose and its contents); a Markdown or text file handed to md from a file manager or another app (ACTION_VIEW, "Open with md"); and text shared into md to start a new document (ACTION_SEND). In every case md only ever receives the item you chose.

Printing, PDF and EPUB

Share Rendered PDF…, Export as PDF… and Print… hand your document to the Android print engine, which produces real A4 pages; pagination is line-aware, so no line of text is sliced across a page boundary (a diagram taller than a page can still be split). A \newpage or \pagebreak on a line of its own starts a fresh page. Printed and exported pages are white with dark ink whichever theme you use. Exporting a book compiles it into a single PDF, or into an EPUB 3 file.

All of this runs on your device. A PDF you share is staged in the app's private cache and handed to the share sheet through a FileProvider, which grants the app you pick a one-off read of that one file and nothing else. Rendering may fetch remote images your document references, exactly as described above; nothing is uploaded.

Third-party services and libraries

No third-party service receives anything. There are no analytics tools (Firebase, Mixpanel, Sentry, etc.), no advertising SDKs (AdMob, Meta, AppLovin, etc.), no crash reporters that phone home, and no attribution providers. md has no back end.

To be exact about what is in the app: md's Kotlin code takes no third-party Gradle dependencies beyond AndroidX / Jetpack Compose and Google's Material components, and the Markdown parser is hand-written. It does, however, bundle several open-source JavaScript engines so that maths and diagrams can be rendered offline inside the preview's web view:

  • KaTeX — mathematical notation.
  • Mermaid — diagrams.
  • Viz.js / Graphviz — graph diagrams.
  • PlantUML — UML diagrams.

These are open-source components shipped inside the app. They execute locally on your device, send nothing anywhere, and nothing about them is downloaded at runtime — the app works with the network switched off, apart from remote images.

The Google Advertising ID is not requested, accessed, or used. md declares this on its Google Play Data Safety form.

Tracking

md does not "track" you in any sense Google Play recognises in its Data Safety taxonomy: it does not link any data collected in the app with data from other apps, websites, or offline sources to build a user profile, and it does not share any data with data brokers. md is therefore declared with Data not collected and Data not shared on its Google Play Data Safety form.

Children's privacy

md is rated for general audiences (Everyone / PEGI 3) and is suitable for all ages. Because md collects no data at all, it collects no data from children. md is a general-purpose utility, not directed at children under 13.

International data transfers

md sends no personal data to us or to anyone on our behalf — we operate no servers and receive nothing — so there are no cross-border transfers by us to disclose under the GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA, LGPD, or similar regimes. The single exception to note for completeness: if your own document references a remote image, your device requests that image directly from the host named in the document, which may be in another country and which will see your IP address, as with any web request you make. That connection is between you and that host; we are not party to it.

Your rights

Because we hold no data about you:

  • There is no record to access under GDPR Article 15 / CCPA "right to know".
  • There is no record to delete under GDPR Article 17 / CCPA "right to delete" (your documents are yours alone — they live where you saved them, and uninstalling md removes the app entirely).
  • There is no record to correct under GDPR Article 16.
  • There is nothing being sold or shared under CCPA / CPRA, so no opt-out is required.

If you'd like confirmation in writing that we hold no data about you, email the address below and we will reply.

Changes to this policy

If a future version of md ever changes any of the above — adds analytics, integrates a third-party SDK, transmits data off-device, or requests new permissions — this document will be updated in the same release and the Effective date at the top will be bumped. The full history is visible in the project's git log on GitHub: github.com/nettrash/md.Android.
